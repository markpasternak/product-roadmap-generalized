# Artist trust and royalty transparency

## What we did
Structured interviews with 42 artists and artist-team members across five markets
and a wide range of career stages, plus a review of 18 months of royalty-related
support contacts.

## What we found

**The amount is not the main complaint.** Artists expect streaming royalties to
be small. What they object to is being unable to check the arithmetic. In 31 of
42 interviews the phrase that recurred was some version of "I just have to trust
it" — and every one of those was said with an edge.

**Most disputes are misunderstandings.** Of the support contacts reviewed, the
clear majority resolved as an explanation rather than a correction: a market rate
difference, a rights split the artist did not know about, a timing offset between
their distributor's statement and ours. Each of those took days of human time to
explain and left the artist no better equipped for next month.

**Distributor statements are the reference point.** Artists reconcile against
their distributor, not against us, and when the two disagree they assume we are
the source of the discrepancy. We currently give them no way to see where the
numbers diverge.

**Opacity generalises.** Artists who distrust royalty reporting also distrust
editorial pitching, promotion reach numbers and analytics. Trust does not
compartmentalise; a single opaque process taxes the whole relationship.

## What it implies
Publishing the derivation — market, rate, split, deduction — is worth more than
increasing the payment. It converts an adversarial exchange into a reconcilable
one, and it removes the repeated human cost of explaining the same five things.

The constraint is that some deduction terms are contractual and cannot be shown.
The recommendation is to publish everything that can be published, and to state
plainly where a line is withheld and why, rather than presenting a partial
derivation as a complete one. A visibly incomplete explanation is recoverable; an
explanation that turns out to have been quietly incomplete is not.
