# Auto-dubbing pilot for high-demand shows

## One-liner
Voice-preserving translated audio tracks for shows with demonstrable demand outside their original language.

## Why it matters
The demand signal is unambiguous — we can see listeners in markets where a show has no localised version consuming it anyway, badly. Dubbing turns catalogue we already have into catalogue that works in markets we are growing in, at a fraction of the cost of commissioning locally. The risk is that a mediocre dub damages the show and the creator relationship, which is why it is a pilot with consent at the centre.

## What ships
A pilot across a small number of consenting shows in three target languages, voice-preserving synthesis with human review before publication, per-show and per-episode creator opt-in with an explicit approval gate, and clear listener labelling that the track is translated.

## Who it's for
Listeners in markets with demand for a show that has no local version; creators who want reach without commissioning.

## Target outcome
A defensible read on whether translated listening retains comparably to native-language listening, and whether creators would opt in at scale.

## Open questions
- Does creator consent extend to the voice likeness, and is our contract language actually sufficient there?
- What is the correction path when a dub gets something materially wrong?
